AuraFlow: a GPU SPH solver for violent free-surface flow

Weakly compressible smoothed-particle hydrodynamics in 2D and 3D, written on NVIDIA Warp and run on a laptop GPU. It is validated against standard dam-break, wave–structure and stream-tunnel benchmarks, differentiable end to end, and fast enough to generate training data for surrogates and reinforcement learning.

In short

  • What: a weakly compressible SPH solver in 2D and 3D on NVIDIA Warp, differentiable end to end, running 24.1 M particle-steps/s on a laptop GPU (DualSPHysics: 20.4 M on the same case and GPU).
  • Validation: SPHERIC Test 2 5/5, Re 100 cylinder 2/2, NACA 0012 4/4, Lobovsky dam break 7/11 (raw impact peaks fail), floating box 5/6. Gates are declared before each run; the one later change (the floating-box metric) is logged on the page.
  • On top: batched runs, a Gaussian-process surrogate with active learning, a Transolver-style field surrogate and SAC reinforcement learning.

Why build it

Commercial and open SPH codes are fast, but they are closed to the things I wanted to try: taking gradients through a simulation, generating thousands of consistent runs for a surrogate, and training a controller inside the solver. AuraFlow started in JAX; a benchmark showed NVIDIA Warp running the same kernels 162–375 times faster than the CPU path, so from v0.2 on it is a Warp-only GPU engine. After the frozen v1.0 campaign, versions v1.1 through v1.2.3 resolved open audit items on wall reaction forces, tunnel confinement, moving-body kinematics, and Mach < 0.1 weakly-compressible scaling.

What is in it

  • Physics chosen by validation. δ-SPH with modified dynamic boundary conditions in 2D; Riemann SPH with density re-initialisation and Adami walls in 3D (still-water density drift +0.07%–0.09% with reinit_relax = 0.3), because that combination passed the 3D gates and the 2D one did not.
  • Exact reaction wall forces (v1.1–v1.2.3). Booking both the kinematic clamp impulse (Δvclamp/Δt) and the Monaghan wall repulsion on rigid bodies closes the fluid–wall momentum balance to 3 × 10−8 of the gravity impulse (down from 0.94% in v1.0). Moving Riemann/Adami walls include g − awall in the wall pressure projection and exact half-step free-body kicks.
  • Open boundaries. A stream tunnel with inflow and outflow buffers (after Tafuni 2018) holds a body in a steady stream for as long as needed. An empty tunnel stays uniform within 0.22% over ten flow-throughs.
  • Speed & memory work, measured. Own sorted cell lists (building the 3D cell list without stream compaction is a further 9% faster in v1.2 with bit-identical trajectories), fused fluid/wall force passes, out-of-domain particle parking (−30% GPU compute time on SPHERIC dx 0.01 m), and CUDA-graph step blocks cut the 3D step time 2.46×. A streaming conservation audit processes one frame at a time, cutting peak RAM from 11 GB to single-frame footprint (~200 MB). A thermal guard keeps the laptop GPU under 87 °C.
  • Differentiable. Gradients of a final-state quantity with respect to viscosity, δ, α, gravity and initial velocities. The forward pass is bit-identical to the production solver; gradients agree with finite differences within 5%, and an inverse run recovers gravity in four gradient evaluations.
  • Batched runs & learning on top. Sixteen small cases share one launch, each bit-identical to its solo run, for 9.3× the throughput. A Gaussian-process surrogate with active learning and conformal 90% intervals, a Transolver-style field surrogate trained on 320 dam breaks (beating nearest-neighbour lookup by 36–47%), and SAC reinforcement learning sit on top.

Validation (v1.0 → v1.2.3)

Tolerances were declared in each case's validation/README.md before the first run, with one explicitly logged post-hoc amendment on the floating-box metric in v1.2.1 (noted below). Since v1.2.2 every run records the gate declarations it started with and validators report gates_changed_since_run. Sound speeds c0 are set so the 99.9th-percentile Mach stays below 0.1 across SPHERIC Test 2 (Mach 0.097), Lobovsky (Mach 0.085–0.098 with Monaghan α scaled by c0,old/c0,new), NACA 0012 at 8° (Mach 0.096), and the 3D CAD pillar (Mach 0.095).

Wave flume passes 4/4, Taylor–Green vortex decay passes 2/2, sphere drag at Re 100 is 1.07 against 1.087 in the literature, and Biswal forced-baffle sloshing passes under both δ-SPH and Riemann once overlapping fluid-on-baffle initial particles were fixed and scored against the linear potential-flow solution (1.143 s period). On NACA 0012, the 4/4 gates apply to α = 0° and 2°; the α = 8° run is an ungated diagnostic (CL 0.147, CD 0.378).

Tunnel cylinder confinement resolved (v1.1–v1.2.2). In v1.0, the Re 100 cylinder Strouhal number sat +6% high (0.174) at both D/dx = 20 and 40. A domain-size matrix (20D vs 40D width, 5D vs 10D inlet) proved the offset was tunnel blockage rather than resolution: in the 40D-wide, 10D-inlet domain at D/dx = 20, both drag (+2.0%) and Strouhal (+1.9%) pass their gates.

What does not pass, and the floating-box gate amendment. Raw impact peak pressures in Lobovsky and sloshing T10 are 5–20× the experimental bands and do not converge with time step — a known limit of single-phase weakly compressible SPH. The Lobovsky front is not monotone in resolution: after the v1.2.3 Mach < 0.1 re-runs (artificial viscosity held fixed), front MAE is 0.016 / 0.014 / 0.037 at dx 0.005 / 0.0025 / 0.00125 m, and the finest front leads the experiment by up to 0.068 x/H, so at that resolution it depends on the artificial viscosity. At D/dx = 10 (the 8 GB GPU ceiling), the 3D sphere wake is dominated by numerical dissipation between Re 100 and 300. On the Hadžić (2005) floating box in waves, the original v1.0 mean zero-up-crossing metric misread small experimental roll wiggles near zero as full cycles (halving apparent experimental roll to 11.6° vs actual ±21.5°) and averaged trailing wave ripples after the packet. In v1.2.1, the gates were amended after results were seen to a peak-to-peak metric over the packet: under those amended gates, v1.2.1 passes 4/6 (heave, sway, roll, and phase lag pass; both wave gauges HN1 and HN2 fail), and v1.2.3 passes 5/6 (HN2 +9.4%, heave +13.9%, sway +9.4%, roll −22.3%, lag 0.018 s pass; HN1 −15.6% fails its ±10% gate).

Reinforcement learning inside the solver

After a literature review (DRLinSPH, FluidGym, HydroGym), the recipe that worked was SAC acting as a residual on a linear expert, with smoothed sensors and free-surface observations, trained on batched tanks.

On a rotating cylinder at Re 100 (HydroGym conventions), SAC trained on a coarse grid and fine-tuned at D/dx 20 reduced drag by 5.8% and lift by 50%. Not everything worked. The published DRLinSPH forced-baffle benchmark was not reproduced (−1.4% against −68.8%), and model-based RL, gradient-based policies through the differentiable solver and a multi-agent wave array all missed their targets in v1.0.

Lessons

  • Declare the tolerance before the run, and log any metric change. One early pressure "near miss" turned out to be a sensor that went dry; on the floating box, tracking gates_changed_since_run keeps the post-hoc peak-to-peak metric amendment transparent.
  • When an error does not move with resolution, check the domain. Doubling cylinder resolution from D/dx = 20 to 40 left Strouhal at +6%; widening the tunnel to 40D with a 10D inlet brought both drag and Strouhal within 2%.
  • Chaotic quantities are not learnable targets. Post-impact pressure varies more between ensemble members than between designs, so the surrogates predict impact time and levels instead.
  • Measure before optimising. Memory reordering bought 7%; splitting the fluid and wall passes bought 2×; dropping stream compaction in the 3D cell list bought another 9% with bit-identical output.
